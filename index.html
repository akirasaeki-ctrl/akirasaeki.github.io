Local Intranet Architecture

This phase analyzes the core structural layouts of local networks, explicitly contrasting packet handling, addressing spaces, and hardware routing versus switching configurations in standard intranet environments.

Router Topology (Layer 3)

Routers connect entirely separate networks and operate at the Network Layer (Layer 3) of the OSI model. They utilize dynamic routing tables and logical IP addresses to dispatch data packets intelligently across wide paths.

Key Attribute: WAN-LAN Routing

Switch Topology (Layer 2)

Switches connect individual end-devices within a singular, localized network at the Data Link Layer (Layer 2). They optimize local frame delivery using hardware MAC addresses, constructing temporary point-to-point connections.

Key Attribute: Low Latency LAN Forwarding

Architectural Analysis: Size and Heat Constraints

As transistor density tracks Moore's classical projection, the chip-making industry encounters severe physical walls, primarily defined by thermal design power (TDP) constraints and the law of sub-threshold leakage currents. Standard silicon gate oxide structures cannot shrink past a single atomic monolayer without prompting quantum tunneling, allowing electrons to seep across off-state switches. Concurrently, reducing size limits concentrates extreme thermal flux in smaller volumes, escalating heat densities to levels exceeding typical commercial heatsink dissipation thresholds. Consequently, computer architecture has shifted from single-core frequency scaling to multi-core arrangements and highly custom hardware accelerators.
